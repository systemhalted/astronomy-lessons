A sidereal day is 23h 56m 04.09s. The 3m 55.91s shortfall against the solar day
is the time Earth needs to turn the extra degree it has travelled along its
orbit since the previous noon. The sidereal day is the real rotation period; the
solar day carries an orbital correction.

Three weeks is 21 nights, so the shift is 21 × 235.91 s = 4954 s = 82.6 minutes
earlier. An object at 40° at 22:00 tonight reaches 40° at roughly 20:37 three
weeks on.

The planets do not obey the rule exactly because they are moving against the
star background on their own account. The four-minute drift is purely Earth's
rotation-versus-orbit bookkeeping and applies cleanly only to objects fixed on
the celestial sphere. A planet's own motion adds to or subtracts from it, which
is why Saturn's rise time slips by a bit more or less than four minutes a night
depending on where it is in its cycle.

Four minutes of clock time is 1.003° of sky rotation — call it one degree.

* Concept: phase angle, and why the Moon is best when it is worst
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: concept
:END:

Phase angle is the angle Sun–object–observer, with its vertex *at the object you
are looking at*. That vertex location is the part people get wrong, and it is
the part that makes the concept useful.

When the phase angle is large, sunlight is arriving nearly sideways relative to
your line of sight. Every bump casts a long shadow across the surface toward
you, and the surface reads as texture and relief. When the phase angle is near
zero, the Sun is almost directly behind your head, light arrives head-on, and
every shadow falls directly behind the object that cast it where you cannot see
it. The surface flattens out into brightness variation with no relief at all.

This is why the full Moon is the worst night of the month for lunar detail. At
full, the phase angle is near zero, the Sun is effectively overhead across the
whole visible disc, and the craters that were spectacular a week earlier have no
shadows to show. The albedo markings are still there — the maria are still dark
— but the topography is gone. The place to work is the terminator, the
day-night boundary, where the Sun is low on the lunar horizon and the phase
angle contribution is extreme.

The same geometry runs the other way for a different effect. Near zero phase
angle, some surfaces get *brighter* than a simple illumination model predicts,
because the shadows that normally reduce the average brightness are all hidden
behind their own particles. This is shadow hiding, and combined with coherent
backscatter it produces the opposition surge, also called the Seeliger effect.
Saturn's rings show it strongly because they are a loose swarm of particles with
a great deal of shadow to hide.

A useful quantitative handle: if you can see a shadow and estimate its length
relative to the height of the thing casting it, you have the local solar
altitude directly.

: tan(local solar altitude) = height / shadow length

A shadow three times the height of its crater wall means the Sun is 18.4° above
that crater's local horizon.

* Exercise: read the terminator twice, and start a brightness log
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: exercise
:END:

** Friday, Sep 18 — colongitude about 3°

The terminator runs near the great crater chain
Ptolemaeus–Alphonsus–Arzachel. Pick one crater, estimate the length of the
shadow its eastern wall throws across the floor as a multiple of the wall
height, and convert with the formula above. You are measuring the Sun's altitude
at a place on another world from your back garden, with an eyepiece and
arithmetic.

** Saturday, Sep 19 — colongitude about 15°

The terminator has moved west and Rupes Recta, the Straight Wall, is on it.
Repeat the shadow measurement. Note that the scarp appears as a stark black line
at this stage. Record that appearance, because the retrieval question below turns
on it.

** Both nights — the brightness log

Saturn reaches opposition on Oct 4–5. Starting tonight, record an estimate of
its brightness every clear night, comparing against a fixed nearby field star so
you have a reference that does not change. The point is to catch the opposition
surge in your own data rather than read about it.
